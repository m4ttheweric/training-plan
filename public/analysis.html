What happened

The ramp that worked

March through mid-May, you built volume steadily: 1-2 runs/week, mixing 3-mile easy days with a weekly 5-6 miler. Pace improved, PRs came, no injuries. This was a good rate.

The ramp that didn't

Late May you jumped to 3x/week and stacked three long runs in 8 days: 6.4 mi, 8.1 mi, 6.3 mi. Weekly volume doubled. Your soleus told you what the research says: the 10% rule exists for a reason.

Heart rate story

Avg HR sits around 145-150 bpm (Z2/Z3 border). Your easy runs aren't easy enough... most are in upper Z2. Slowing your easy pace by 30-45 sec/mi would keep you in true Z2 and reduce injury risk.

Strength balance

You're lifting consistently: squat, deadlift, bench on a solid rotation. This is a major advantage for injury prevention. The soleus issue likely came from volume spike, not weakness.

Key rules
Never increase weekly mileage by more than 10-15%. Never increase distance AND frequency in the same week. Add a recovery week (drop 25%) every 4th week. If the soleus talks, listen: drop back one phase for a week.
Easy pace
Your easy runs should be 9:30-10:00/mi... slow enough to hold a full conversation. Your current "easy" pace of ~9:15 pushes into Z3. True easy running builds aerobic base without accumulating tissue stress.
Long run
Keep the long run at conversational pace. It's about time on feet, not speed. Walk hills if needed. The long run should never be more than ~35-40% of your weekly total.
Soleus care
Add 2x daily standing bent-knee calf raises (soleus-specific): 3 sets of 15, slow eccentric. These are the single best preventive exercise for your specific injury history.
